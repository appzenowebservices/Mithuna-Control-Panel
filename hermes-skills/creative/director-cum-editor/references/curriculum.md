# DIRECTOR-CUM-EDITOR SKILLS

## A Practical Cinema Curriculum for AstroLogics

> **Core principle**
>
> A director decides **what the audience experiences**.
>
> An editor decides **when the audience experiences it**.
>
> The director-editor learns to control both.

---

# 0. THE MASTER PIPELINE

Every audiovisual work passes through this loop:

```text
IDEA
 ↓
MEANING
 ↓
STORY
 ↓
SCRIPT
 ↓
VISUAL CONCEPT
 ↓
SHOT DESIGN
 ↓
STORYBOARD / SHOT LIST
 ↓
TIMELINE
 ↓
BLENDER / LIVE ACTION / ASSETS
 ↓
EDIT
 ↓
SOUND DESIGN
 ↓
MUSIC
 ↓
VFX / MOTION GRAPHICS
 ↓
COLOR
 ↓
MIX
 ↓
MASTER
```

But this is NOT linear.

The real process is:

```text
        ┌──────────────┐
        │    STORY     │
        └──────┬───────┘
               ↓
        ┌──────────────┐
        │   TIMELINE   │
        └──────┬───────┘
               ↓
        ┌──────────────┐
        │   VISUALS    │
        └──────┬───────┘
               ↓
        ┌──────────────┐
        │    SOUND     │
        └──────┬───────┘
               ↓
        ┌──────────────┐
        │   REVIEW     │
        └──────┬───────┘
               │
               └──────────→ STORY
```

The timeline is the central laboratory.

# 4. DIRECTING

For every shot decide:

### What should the viewer notice?

### When should they notice it?

### What should they feel?

# 8. THE LANGUAGE OF CUTTING

Learn why different cuts exist.

## Hard cut

Change information immediately.

## Jump cut

Create discontinuity, compression or instability.

## Match cut

Connect two visually or conceptually related images.

## Cutaway

Redirect attention or hide time.

## Reaction shot

Show consequence.

## Insert

Show important detail.

## Montage

Compress time or build an idea through association.

## Cross-cutting

Connect simultaneous events.

# 9. TEMPORAL CONTROL

This is one of the most important director-editor skills.

Learn to control:

```text
SHOT DURATION
↓
SCENE DURATION
↓
SEQUENCE DURATION
↓
FILM RHYTHM
```

Experiment with:

```text
0.5 sec
1 sec
2 sec
4 sec
8 sec
15 sec
```

The same visual can feel:

* exciting
* threatening
* peaceful
* confusing
* boring
* profound

depending on duration.

---

# 10. PACING

Pacing is not simply "fast editing."

Learn:

* acceleration
* deceleration
* anticipation
* pause
* release
* escalation
* rhythmic repetition
* interruption
* silence
* breath

A slow shot can be extremely intense.

A fast sequence can be emotionally empty.

---

# 11. J-CUTS AND L-CUTS

Master these early.

### J-cut

```text
AUDIO B
    ↓
IMAGE B
```

The sound arrives before the visual.

### L-cut

```text
IMAGE A
    ↓
AUDIO A continues
        ↓
IMAGE B
```

The previous sound continues into the next shot.

These create continuity and psychological flow.

# 23. EDITING WORKFLOW

Never begin with:

> "Let's make the final shot."

Begin with:

```text
SCRIPT
 ↓
SHOT LIST
 ↓
ROUGH TIMELINE
 ↓
TEMP AUDIO
 ↓
BLENDER PLACEHOLDERS
 ↓
ROUGH CUT
```

Then create final visuals.

This prevents wasting days rendering shots that later get cut.

---

# 24. THE ROUGH CUT

Your first objective:

> Make the story work with ugly footage.

Use:

* placeholders
* low-resolution renders
* screenshots
* primitive Blender scenes
* temporary music
* temporary SFX

If the story doesn't work here, beautiful rendering won't save it.

---

# 25. THE FINE CUT

After the structure works:

* tighten cuts
* remove unnecessary shots
* adjust shot duration
* improve transitions
* improve pacing
* refine emotional beats
* synchronize sound
* improve visual continuity

---

# 26. PICTURE LOCK

Eventually stop changing the visual edit.

Then:

```text
PICTURE LOCK
 ↓
FINAL SOUND
 ↓
FINAL VFX
 ↓
COLOR
 ↓
MASTER
```

Constantly changing the edit while mixing final audio is a splendid way to manufacture suffering.

Use Blender for:

```text
MODELING
RIGGING
ANIMATION

```

Use the editor for:

```text

```

Do not force Blender to do everything.

Do not force the editor to do everything.

# 28. PROXY WORKFLOW

For heavy footage:

```text

      ↓
LOW-RES PROXY
      ↓
```

# 31. DIRECTOR'S REVIEW

After every version, watch the entire piece without touching the timeline.

Write down:

```text
WHERE DID I GET BORED?
WHERE DID I GET CONFUSED?
WHERE DID I LOOK AWAY?
WHERE DID I FEEL SOMETHING?
WHERE DID I EXPECT SOMETHING?
WHERE DID THE SOUND FEEL WRONG?
WHERE DID THE STORY STOP MOVING?
```

Then fix those points.

---

# 32. EDITOR'S REVIEW

Ask:

### Story

Does every scene contribute?

### Pacing

Is anything too long?

### Clarity

Does the audience understand enough?

### Emotion

Does the emotional progression work?

### Continuity

Does the spatial logic work?

### Sound

Can everything important be heard?

### Visuals

Is the eye going where it should?

### Rhythm

Does image + sound form one rhythm?

---

# 33. DIRECTOR'S PRINCIPLE

Never ask:

> "Is this shot cool?"

Ask:

> "Does this shot belong?"

A beautiful shot that doesn't belong is still a bad shot.

---

# 34. EDITOR'S PRINCIPLE

Learn to kill your own work.

If a 20-second shot works better as 5 seconds:

**cut it.**

If an expensive Blender sequence doesn't help the story:

**cut it.**

If the music is beautiful but wrong:

**cut it.**

If the effect took three days and contributes nothing:

**delete it.**

The timeline does not care how long something took to make.

Ultimately:

```text
                 DIRECTOR
                    │
        ┌───────────┼───────────┐
        ↓           ↓           ↓
      STORY       IMAGE       SOUND
        │           │           │
        ↓           ↓           ↓
    SCREENPLAY   BLENDER      AUDIO
        │           │           │
        └───────────┼───────────┘
                    ↓
                 EDITING
                    ↓
                 TIMELINE
                    ↓
               COLOR / MIX
                    ↓
                  FILM
```

The timeline is where all three languages meet:

```text
STORY
IMAGE
SOUND
```

That is why editing is the next major skill.

# 39. THE FINAL GOAL

Do not aim to become:

> Blender expert + Kdenlive expert + audio expert + VFX expert.

Aim to become:

> **Someone capable of taking an abstract idea and turning it into a controlled audiovisual experience.**

Software is interchangeable.

The underlying skills are not.

Your final capability should be:

```text
IDEA
 ↓
MEANING
 ↓
STORY
 ↓
IMAGE
 ↓
MOVEMENT
 ↓
TIME
 ↓
SOUND
 ↓
EMOTION
 ↓
EXPERIENCE
```

That is the director-cum-editor.

And AstroLogics gives you an unusually good laboratory for learning it because you have a potentially endless source of stories: **the changing sky → symbolic interpretation → human experience → audiovisual expression.**
